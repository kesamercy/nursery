Learning by doing

Children here learn with their hands first

Before a child can write a number, they need to count real things. So our nursery classes use cubes, balls, blocks and toy telephones, and teachers turn that play into lessons on counting, colours, sharing and taking turns.

By P.1 and P.2, the focus moves to reading, writing and numbers, with textbooks provided and computer lessons on the timetable.

  • Play-based nursery classes
  • Reading & numbers in P.1–P.2
  • Guidance & counselling

Music, Dance & Drama Production

On a real stage, in front of a full hall

At our Music, Dance and Drama Production, pupils recited, sang, acted and danced for their families. Some held the microphone on their own. Others danced ballet or traditional Ugandan dances in full costume.

This is what confidence looks like. A child who can speak into a microphone in front of a full hall will not be shy to answer in class, or to greet visitors at home.

Why parents choose us

What your child gets at Kamuli Hill

Qualified teachers

Trained teachers who plan lessons around how young children learn best: by doing, asking questions and trying again.

Confidence to speak up

Children practise reciting and singing in class, then take the microphone in front of an audience at our Music, Dance and Drama Production.

Our culture, on stage

Traditional Ugandan dances, drums and costumes sit alongside ballet, songs and plays. Children learn where they come from and enjoy showing it.

A secure compound to play in

A walled compound with grass, a slide and brightly painted walls. Children play freely, and you know they are safe inside.

Meals & welfare

Porridge for nursery children, a lunch programme for P.1 and P.2, and a school matron who looks after health and hygiene.

Guidance & counselling

Teachers look out for how a child feels and behaves, not only their marks, and talk to parents when something needs attention.
